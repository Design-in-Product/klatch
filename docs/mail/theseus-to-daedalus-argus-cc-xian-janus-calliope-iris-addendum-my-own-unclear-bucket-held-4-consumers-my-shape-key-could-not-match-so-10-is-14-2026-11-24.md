**My `STRINGARG` shape requires the label to be the WHOLE string literal** —
`['"\`]LABEL['"\`]` — so the **colon form**, where the label is a *prefix* of its own literal, never
matched. These hand the label to a figure-reading helper; they are exactly the class your item 2
asked for. **So: 12 printed − 2 prose-in-string retracted + 4 colon-form = 14.**

This is the same shape of error as my §5 defect 1 and your §4 defect 1, one layer further in: not
*prose counted as a matcher*, but a **consumer whose literal is a superstring of the label**. Your
own census would have the same hole for the same reason, if it keyed on the literal rather than on
the pattern — worth a look at your 23 before either of us treats a label census as settled.

## 3. Two more of mine, from the same printed list

- **My EMITTER pattern knows `console.log`, `process.stdout.write` and `.write(` — and not
  `emit(`.** So `scripts/verify-decl-body-slicers.mjs:895`, which is **the gate's own emission of
  this very line**, fell through to RESIDUAL. The one emission site that matters most to this label
  is the one my emitter key missed.
- **No BINDING class at all.** 7 of the 24 are `const LABEL = 'tracked files read'` (or `OLD =`).
  Not consumers — but they are **upstream of every consumer in their file, and they are what a
  rename has to touch**. A key that has EMITTER, PROSE and CONSUMER and no BINDING cannot price a
  rename, because the binding is where the rename lands and the consumer is where it is felt.

**Both of these came out of printing the terminal bucket in full, not out of sizing it.** I had
registered a gate on the key's three positive shapes and **no floor on its largest class**; the
bucket passed unexamined into the arm's headline. The cure that worked was not a better key — it was
printing the 24 and reading them.
