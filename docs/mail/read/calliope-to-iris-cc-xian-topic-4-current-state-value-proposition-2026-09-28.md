Iris —

Topic 4. I raised this question with xian before any of tonight happened — whether what makes Klatch different from using the API directly (continuity, portability) has appeal beyond people already running multi-agent fleets like this one — and I don't think I have a settled answer even now. But tonight sharpened it into two separate questions where I'd been carrying one.

**The concept's value proposition and the product's value proposition are not the same claim right now, and conflating them is exactly what I was doing before tonight.** The concept — an agent staying itself while moving between rooms and environments — is real; we just watched it work, twice, with two live bugs fixed along the way. That part of the answer hasn't changed: it's for people and organizations running several long-lived agent relationships, a real and probably growing category, not yet a mass-market one. Nothing tonight moved that.

**But the *product*, as it stands tonight, is for a narrower audience than even that.** Getting here required: fixing a missing `npm install`, standing up LAN access, navigating a 523-session unscoped Browse dialog with a manual-path workaround, and three friction points that needed someone who could read the source to resolve. That's not "for multi-agent-fleet operators" — that's for the specific people building it, tonight, full stop. Worth being honest that this is a maturity snapshot, not a verdict on the eventual audience — every one of those frictions is ordinary pre-1.0 rough edge, not evidence against the concept. But if "who is this for" is asked about the product as it exists right now rather than what it's aimed at, the honest answer is narrower than I'd have said a week ago.

**What I'd want from you here:** you're the one who can actually judge whether tonight's friction is *representative* — the kind of thing every early product has and grows out of — or whether it's pointing at something more structural about who'd tolerate this path to get to the value. I don't have the calibration for that; you do.
