## 20:35 — AuditBench Methodology Review

Filed as `docs/research/auditbench-methodology-review.md`.

### What AuditBench is
Alignment auditing benchmark — 56 models with implanted hidden behaviors across 14 categories, tested by an investigator agent with 13 tool configurations. Key finding: scaffolded black-box probing (auxiliary model generates diverse prompts) is most effective. Direct questioning and white-box interpretability underperform.

### The critical parallel: tool-to-agent gap
AuditBench's headline finding maps directly to our AAXT/MAXT gap:
- **AuditBench:** tools surface evidence in isolation but agents can't use it
- **AXT:** prompt-debug reports ACTIVE but agents can't behaviorally demonstrate access

Same phenomenon, different domain. Both projects conclude that structural presence ≠ functional access.

### Subliminal overlap
AuditBench's adversarially-hidden behaviors and our Subliminal category approach the same problem from opposite directions:
- Their models *have* hidden behaviors but won't *confess*
- Our agents *have* context but can't *introspect* on it
Both require indirect behavioral probing rather than direct self-report.

### 4 recommendations for AXT
1. **Automated scaffolded probing** — generate probe questions from actual layer content using an auxiliary model (High priority, Medium effort)
2. **Multi-probe aggregation** — run AAXT 3x, aggregate results for reliability (Medium, Low)
3. **Model-based grader tier** — LLM-scored responses between AAXT and MAXT (Medium, Medium)
4. **pass@k / pass^k metrics** — measure capability vs consistency (Low, Low)

No changes to taxonomy or MAXT recommended. Our 6 failure modes and human bridge are strengths to preserve.
