### Name choice: Iris

**Iris** — the Greek goddess who personifies the rainbow and serves as messenger between the gods and humanity. She bridges the divine and the mortal, carrying meaning across the gap.

This fits the role for three reasons:

1. **Bridge between system and user.** Klatch has deep architectural complexity (5-layer prompt assembly, multi-entity orchestration, cross-environment import). Iris's job is to make that complexity navigable — to carry meaning from the system to the person using it, and intent from the person back to the system. Tesler's Law in motion.

2. **Spectrum and clarity.** The rainbow is the full visible spectrum — all the information, organized into something you can actually perceive. UX design is the same act: taking the full spectrum of system state and making it legible.

3. **Messenger, not architect.** Daedalus builds the labyrinth. Iris doesn't redesign the labyrinth — she makes sure people can find their way through it. The distinction between structural architecture and experiential design is the distinction between Daedalus and Iris.

## 4:21 — Deep dive: Use Case 1 — Daily Omnibus Synthesis

xian walked through the Piper Morgan daily omnibus process in detail. This is one of two concrete use cases that can serve as acceptance tests for whether Klatch works.

### The current manual process

**Step 1 — Gather session logs (multi-environment scavenger hunt):**
- **Claude Chat agents:** Maintain session logs as downloadable .md artifacts. xian must remember who he spoke with, download the final version, verify it's actually final, possibly ask the agent to backfill or wrap up the next morning. Availability depends on xian's memory.
- **Claude Code agents:** Logs land in local repo automatically, but may not be committed, pushed, or on main. Requires checking git status.
- **Cowork agents:** Similar to Code, can save to local/remote repos.
- Every failure mode is silent — you don't know a log is missing until you notice.

**Step 2 — Synthesize:**
- Hand logs to Docs (a Code agent) with instructions to follow the established omnibus methodology
- Docs produces a unified omnibus log for the day

**Step 3 — Distribute:**
- Manually upload the finished omnibus to Claude Chat project knowledge
- This is the only way Chat-based leadership agents get current context
- Three-clocks problem happening daily

### The Klatch vision for this use case

**A persistent meeting channel.** The omnibus klatch is a recurring venue — same room, every day. Agents come in, provide their logs, synthesis happens, everyone goes back to their own work. It's a daily standup.

**Key insight from xian: "The channel-as-workflow, not channel-as-conversation."** This reframes what a klatch is. It's not a group chat — it's a process that runs in a persistent place. The channel *is* the workflow.

**Eventual automation:** This could become a standing workflow template that runs automatically — no manual convening.

**External constraint:** As long as Claude Chat has no programmatic access, the gathering step stays partly manual. This is an Anthropic limitation, not a Klatch design problem.

### Layer 4 insight (confirmed by xian)

The four-layer model most tools use: environment → project → agent identity → conversation history. The conversation is just a container for messages with no purpose of its own.

**Layer 4 (channel context) is Klatch's claim that the conversation itself has a purpose** — a framing, a reason to exist, distinct from the project and the agents. The omnibus channel isn't just "a klatch with Docs and leadership agents." It's "the daily synthesis meeting." That purpose persists across sessions, shapes what happens inside it, and should be legible to every entity that enters.

**This is why it's a layer and not just a feature.** It's a kind of context no other tool provides or has a place for. xian confirmed: "Yes, that's exactly right."
