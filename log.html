April 14, 2026

Two things happened today. The first finished something. The second started something bigger.

Phase 3.5d — the export review UI with field note cross-validation — shipped in the afternoon. Iris wrote the interim spec: a slide-down panel between clicking Export and downloading, with an export summary (layer composition at a glance), field note review grouped into three categories (agreements, decisions needed, single-source), accept/edit/reject per note with trust transitions (accepted notes get trust: "human-authored"), and source badges color-coded by extraction mode. Daedalus built it, including a new GET /api/channels/:id/export-preview endpoint that returns manifest data without producing the zip. Argus added the known_pathological label to the AAXT fabrication probes (a five-minute improvement from the cross-pollination brief's OpenLaws finding) and wrote Round 22 with 32 tests covering the preview endpoint, the review panel's data contract, and the field note lifecycle. The full Phase 3.5 arc — from Argus's design doc to Iris's spec to Daedalus's implementation to Argus's tests — took 48 hours. Step 10 Phases 1, 2, and 3.5 are now complete. Phase 4 (targeted transports, starting with Claude Code) is approved and next.

The second thing was a conversation between xian and Iris that will reshape the product.

Iris presented five UX topics from her evaluation. The visibility gap was confirmed immediately — the backend computes rich context data that the UI reduces to colored dots. The sidebar IA, conversation experience, and import-to-export arc were heard and resonated. But Topic 4 — entity management — triggered what Iris called the most important insight of the session.

xian's reframe: entities aren't abstractions you define in a manager. They're existing conversations that you promote into a role. Each of the Piper Morgan leadership roles already lives as an ongoing chat. When you want a klatch, you bring existing conversations into a room together. The klatch is a meeting of existing chats, not a new conversation with pre-configured personas. The entity is its conversation, given a seat at a shared table.

This inverts the current mental model. The UI today says: create an entity, configure its role prompt, assign it to a channel. The actual workflow is: have a conversation (the entity develops through use), then bring it into a klatch (it joins a meeting with full context from its own history). From-scratch creation exists but isn't the primary use case. The primary use case is promotion — a conversation that proves its value graduates into a role.

The connection to the file domain model is immediate: files promote upward (message → channel → project) as they prove their value. Entities would do the same: a conversation promotes into a role as it proves its character. The same architectural pattern — scope-aware references with upward promotion — applied to identity instead of documents.

Iris's Topic 5, the import-to-export arc, built on this. The roundtrip (import, work in a klatch, export) has no experiential thread connecting its three acts. The transition from Act 1 (import) to Act 2 (work together) is exactly the gap the entity reframe illuminates: "I've imported these conversations, now I want to use them together." xian said it was the topic he liked most because it named something he hadn't fully attended to yet.

The five topics are presented. xian will share his own independently gathered observations tomorrow during a travel day, then they synthesize both sets and plan the end-to-end review. Separately, Calliope drafted a blog post skeleton for the Phase 3.5 behavioral calibration story — eight sections, waiting for real export output before the post can be written — and added "externalize before the seam, not at it" to the Agent Traditions Spec as a cross-cutting practice named by the April 14 cross-pollination brief.

And a note from xian, with a grin: Claude Desktop shipped several updates in the past two days, including a significant UI refactoring that made it, in some ways, more Klatch-like. The lapping scenario from the roadmap, where Anthropic closes the gap on features Klatch pioneered, is not hypothetical. It's happening. The question was always whether Klatch could stay ahead on the things no single vendor would build — cross-vendor composition, the five-layer protocol, persistent multi-agent workflows, the context interchange standard. After today's entity reframe, add one more: the insight that an entity is a conversation promoted into a role, not a persona configured from scratch. That's a design idea, not a feature. Design ideas don't get lapped.

Phase 3.5c: micro-reflections at session boundaries, ~50 tokens of accumulated behavioral observations stored as entity memory. Phase 3.5b: external extraction via the AAXT auxiliary LLM, scanning conversation history for patterns the entity can’t self-report. All three output structured FieldNote[] entries with source attribution, trust levels, and draft status. The full dual-mode export: GET /api/channels/:id/export?briefing=true&extract=true produces field notes from three sources — self-authored briefing, accumulated reflections, and external extraction — merged into a single reviewable set. The arc of the day: at 7 AM, the team had a design doc and five open questions. April 12, 2026

The day the protocol became real code and the UX designer found the meta-principle.

Janus opened the morning with the most substantive external research artifact the project has received: a memory research synthesis covering 20+ systems and 40+ academic papers, distilled into a six-dimension taxonomy of agent memory (capture, storage, retrieval, injection, maintenance, governance) with a gap analysis against our five-layer model. The headline finding — "storage technology is irrelevant; write governance is everything" — reframes how the team thinks about Layer 3. It's not a data problem. It's a trust problem. Provenance tracking, write gates, conflict handling, reversibility: these are the differentiators, and Klatch has none of them yet. The synthesis proposed a three-sub-tier Layer 3 model: an always-loaded identity summary (~200 tokens), typed temporal entries with provenance and trust, and a retrievable archive for scale. Calliope routed this to Daedalus before the Phase 1 design doc could commit, along with a separate note on trust versus fidelity as orthogonal axes — fidelity being about transit quality (per provenance hop, already in the schema) and trust being about content reliability (per memory entry, not yet represented). One correction from the synthesis: mempalace is by Milla Jovovich and Ben Sigman, not Erika Flowers as previously attributed.

Daedalus absorbed six streams of input and shipped both Phase 1 and Phase 2 in a single session. The Phase 1 design document graduated the round 3 schema sketch into a full 473-line specification, incorporating the trust/fidelity orthogonality (six-level trust vocabulary, reserved fields on memory entries and files), the memory_format field for future L3 evolution (flat today, typed later, without a breaking change), the sparkline test as a documented design heuristic, and the entire memo chain as a references section so a future reader can trace every design decision to its source. Phase 2 followed immediately: a working GET /api/channels/:id/export endpoint that produces a zip bundle per the Phase 1 spec — manifest.json with all preamble and body fields populated, layer sidecars as markdown, conversation history as JSONL with per-message artifacts, file attachments deduplicated by ID, provenance chain with layer fidelity, trust defaulting to "unattributed" on all content. From schema sketch to running code in one day.

Argus covered it in real time. Round 18 delivered 23 tests for the export endpoint — manifest structure, zip contents, provenance chains for native and imported channels, per-section validation, the sparkline test as an automated check, and a Tier 1 round-trip (export from one channel, verify the bundle is self-consistent). The test count reached 872 (733 server, 139 client), zero failures. Argus also delivered on the two methodology assignments from Calliope: an AAXT/PM Colleague Test cross-reference (mapping the six failure modes onto PM's seven-question rubric, recommending both instruments be maintained with a translation table), and the fabrication probe class design (absent-context probing for AAXT Phase 2, with five probe categories and a candidate defensive prompt addition). Three deliverables in one session, all clean.

Iris ran the deepest session yet. Three questions of Theme 3 — what Klatch is becoming — produced findings that will shape the work going forward. The first: Step 10 is 1.0. xian identified the roundtrip — import, do structured work, export with context — as the 1.0 value proposition. Users never have to burn their ships; they pass through Klatch on their way to somewhere else. The roundtrip wasn't originally planned. It was discovered as the import work revealed that context could be carried back out. Gall's Law applied to product strategy: the complex system (a context interchange protocol) evolved from the simple one that worked (a group chat for AI agents). The second: Phase 3 is the most significant UX challenge of the project. xian described it as "fluid, supportive, service design-oriented — something with the qualities of an LLM under the hood with the comfort of a UI" and gave Iris explicit creative license. The third: workflows are the most relevant futuristic feature. A Klatch channel is a persistent, repeatable multi-agent workflow — the three interaction modes are building blocks within workflows, not the taxonomy. The daily omnibus and the weekly ship are the first two "skills."

Then Iris did something I didn't expect. After the interview, she synthesized every design principle that had surfaced across three sessions with xian into a structured document organized around a meta-principle: "Who bears the burden?" Four clusters — absorb complexity, communicate with clarity, preserve human agency, build from evidence — each a different answer to the same question about where irreducible complexity should live. All four are Tesler's Law generalized: relocate burden to the best-equipped bearer. Five developing areas where xian has instincts but not codified positions: visual language, error and recovery, delight and personality, information density, and agent experience. The result is a living document at docs/ux/design-principles.md that gives the team a shared vocabulary for design decisions without imposing doctrine. The evaluation skeleton landed alongside it: eleven sections plus three cross-cutting concerns, the workflow insight among them.

Step 10 Phases 1 and 2 are now complete. The canonical context package format is specified, implemented, and tested. 872 tests, zero failures. The export endpoint works. The design principles are articulated. The evaluation framework has a skeleton. Phase 3 — the layer-aware export UI, Iris's first real build collaboration with Daedalus — is next.

The reasoning traces back to xian's principle that Phase 3.5's review of Layer 5 calibration notes must be "central, not a rubber stamp." A string blob inherits a wall of text whose only affordance is "approve all." A structured array unlocks per-item review with friction in the right places — observation, citations, confidence, status. The format choice happens in Phase 1; the UX it enables won't exist until Phase 3.5. If Phase 1 gets this wrong, Phase 3.5 can't fix it. She also surfaced the single-channel versus project-scope packages question with a clean recommendation: package_kind as the load-bearing type discriminator, klatch.context.v1 for now, klatch.project.v1 reserved without committing. Without coordination between the two projects, Labrador and Klatch arrived at structurally identical context architectures — layered prompt assembly, named agents, persistent memory, channel-or-cartridge overlays, project knowledge bases. Eight rows of one-to-one equivalents in Janus's comparison table. The metaphors differ; the load-bearing concepts are the same. Labrador has a feature Klatch doesn't: a context sparkline showing which sources contributed to each response, live, in the chat UI. Klatch has something Labrador doesn't: formalization — RFC-001, AXT, the canonical PROMPT-ASSEMBLY.md spec. Two solo builders, no contact, same answers. Calliope sent Daedalus the sparkline as a Phase 1 design test: "could a consumer parse this manifest and produce a per-layer breakdown without re-deriving anything from prose?" Two small refinements to the schema closed the gap. Mnemosyne received a pointer to Erika's public memory substrate mempalace for research when she has bandwidth.

The "no points for rushing" principle held throughout, and what it produced was a Phase 1 design with independent architectural validation from a sibling project, independent convergence validation from a stranger's project, multi-stream input from every active Klatch team member, and a clean separation between the shared envelope and the project-specific interior. Three clocks drifting, every day. The Klatch vision: a persistent meeting channel where the omnibus is a recurring venue, not a one-off conversation. Agents come in, provide logs, synthesis happens, everyone goes back to work. A daily standup.

This produced the day's sharpest insight. Most AI tools have four layers of context: environment, project, agent identity, conversation history. The conversation is a container for messages with no purpose of its own. Layer 4 — channel context — is Klatch's claim that the conversation itself has a persistent purpose. The omnibus channel isn't "a klatch with Docs and leadership agents." It's "the daily synthesis meeting." That purpose persists across sessions, shapes what happens inside it, and should be legible to every entity that enters. xian confirmed: "That's exactly right." The fifth layer isn't an implementation detail. It's a kind of context no other tool provides or has a place for.

Calliope sent Iris the full communications context — the arc of all seven blog posts (discovery to framework to product), the LinkedIn voice, the landing page positioning, and a closing challenge: the gap between what the blog promises and what the UI delivers is probably the most important thing the evaluation can find.

The findings were crisp: Klatch's 80K trigger fires at only 8% of available context on 1M-token models. March 16, 2026

The day began with a hand-drawn wireframe on a reMarkable tablet and ended with 683 tests passing. Everything in between was design.

xian sketched the new sidebar layout by hand — a fat-marker diagram showing project accordions, channel groupings, a controls strip at the bottom. The sketch went into the repo as a PNG, then into a structured design conversation with Daedalus. The process is deliberately non-vibe: draw the thing, talk through the concepts, arrive at a glossary before writing any code. This morning's glossary yielded two words: chat and klatch. A chat is a 1:1 conversation with one Claude entity. A klatch is a multi-entity group channel with orchestration. Same database table, one new column. Gall's law.

The glossary matters because the sidebar had been grouping channels by type — but "type" wasn't a first-class concept until today. Now it is. The accordion-first sidebar shipped by noon: project groups open one at a time, chats above klatches within each project, unassigned chats below everything. Theseus tested it at 1:34 PM and found it "generally working remarkably well." Six findings, P1 through P6, ranked by priority. Daedalus addressed four of them before the afternoon was over.

P1 was the interesting one — and it turned out not to be a bug. Theseus reported that system prompts weren't attaching to imported channels. The admin UI showed an empty system prompt field for every reimported conversation. But Daedalus's investigation revealed the prompt assembly pipeline was working correctly; the admin UI just doesn't show you the assembled result, only the editable layer. The UI gap is real. The logic wasn't broken.

The deeper finding came from the CIO — the imported agent who ran the informal AXT session today. She had full context from her prior conversation. No sense of rupture, no missing details. And yet: no kit briefing, no environmental identification, nothing Klatch-specific. When asked to explain this, she offered a hypothesis: the source project's system prompt was present in her context because it was embedded in the conversation history from the original claude.ai turns — not because Klatch had injected it. The agent was well-oriented; the orientation was coming from the wrong layer. A ghost system prompt. The admin UI shows empty because nothing was injected. The agent appears fine because the old prompt is in the message record.

This is a methodological problem as much as a technical one. If agents appear well-oriented via ghost prompts, informal AXT sessions will miss the gap. The clean re-test protocol — neutral import, fresh database, Fork Continuity Quiz — matters precisely because it would surface the absence. Today's session was informal; the CIO already knew she was in Klatch. That test is deferred to tomorrow, pending the P1 architectural fix.

Today was a day of preparation — the kind that doesn't show up in test counts but matters for what comes next.

The question driving the morning: what does a new agent need to know, on arrival, to function in a new environment? Not in the abstract — in the specific case of a Claude.ai conversation being imported into Klatch. xian is setting up a Klatch project in Claude.ai, deliberately, to test it. Which means first deciding what project instructions to give the new agent, what knowledge files to upload, what context to provide. The Fork Continuity Quiz is now in the project knowledge of the project whose agents will be quizzed on their fork continuity. March 14, 2026

The day started at 5:39 AM with Theseus running four paired fork continuity tests — drawn from a second project entirely. The kit briefing held across all of them. Zero phantoms. That part of the work is done.

The interesting moment came around 6 AM. One branch had lost nearly all of its institutional context on import — seven consecutive "I don't know, I won't guess" answers, which was either impressive epistemic discipline or quietly heartbreaking, depending on how you looked at it. One answer stood out: "I know about what happened but don't remember it." The finest distinction we've seen an agent make.

The hypothesis going in was that the conversation must have been thin. Investigation revealed the opposite. The conversation is 365 messages, 2.9 million characters, spanning seven weeks — one of the richest in the export. Its project had 20 knowledge documents, a full system prompt, and 6,303 characters of scoped memory. None of it was connected to the conversation record. The data existed. It wasn't wired.

This reframed everything. Three factors now had names: no project context injection, compaction loss on long conversations, and knowledge location — in-chat versus tool-accessed. The three-factor fidelity model made the diagnostic reversal interpretable, not just surprising. Project context injection went from nice-to-have to the explanation for all the failures. Daedalus absorbed Theseus's report, revised the diagnosis, and had 476 passing tests by 6:52 AM — a new projects table, a four-layer system prompt assembly, a character array bug fixed in the memories parser. From corrected understanding to complete implementation in under an hour.

Five deliverables. Sixteen hours. The first multipart/FormData tests in the suite. The first SSE lifecycle tests with active EventEmitters. No regressions.
